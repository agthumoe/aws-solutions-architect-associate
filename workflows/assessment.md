# Diagnostic, practice and readiness

## Diagnostic
Use 20 ORIGINAL scenario questions: D1=6, D2=5, D3=5, D4=4. Include single-answer and multiple-response questions (state how many options to select). This distribution is a local approximation to official weights. Ask one at a time; wait for the answer and optional confidence. Explain the correct decision and each distractor afterward. Track numerator/denominator per primary domain and save a checkpoint after every answer. Ask no questions as part of the reset itself.

## Quizzes and recall
Default quiz: five scenario questions, one at a time with feedback. Brief recall drills may use simpler prompts. Record original/generated practice separately from official or reputable external calibration. Label repetitions and hints; do not inflate fresh accuracy with them. No leaked exams or proprietary question reproduction. For multi-select use exact-match scoring without partial credit as a local practice convention.

## Full mock
65 original questions, suggested 130-minute timer, approximately D1=20/D2=17/D3=15/D4=13. Score all 65 for local practice. Do not pretend to identify 15 unscored questions. Default one question at a time; feedback is withheld until submission/end, with batching only if requested. Have the learner run a timer if the tool cannot reliably enforce it. Persist actual answers and pending question; never include an answer key in a visible prompt or learner checkpoint. At the end explain answers and alternatives, record raw percentage and domain counts, and schedule errors/guesses. Do not convert percentages to AWS scaled scores or claim a generated mock predicts an official pass.

## Readiness
Report insufficient evidence until fresh results exist. Do not calculate readiness from arbitrary note-status points. Show fresh/unaided accuracy with denominators by domain, delayed recall, unresolved mistakes, full task coverage and timed mock results. State small-sample limitations.
A provisional study target is at least two fresh, reputable timed full mocks around 80–85%+, no persistent major domain gaps, and successful delayed explanations of high-value trade-offs. This is a coaching heuristic, not an AWS requirement, pass probability, or guarantee. Repeated questions and AI-generated quizzes alone do not establish exam readiness. If external mocks are unavailable, explicitly report that missing calibration.

AWS uses a scaled 100–1000 score with a minimum passing score of 720; there is no valid local percentage conversion. Official exam details and weights: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/aws-certification/latest/solutions-architect-associate-03/solutions-architect-associate-03.html (checked 2026-11-08).
